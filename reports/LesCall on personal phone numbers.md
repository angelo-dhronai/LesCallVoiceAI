# Forward the number, keep the DID hidden

**A LesCall agent can answer a user's personal number today through carrier call forwarding to a dedicated rented DID, and that is the only method that is implementable now, keeps the number working on the phone and has no prohibition that this research could find.** It is inbound only. It also keeps a Plivo number behind the scenes, so "not Plivo numbers" is true from the user's and caller's view and false from the platform's: the DID, its rental, Plivo's KYC and its India rules all remain. Outbound calls showing the personal number cannot be placed over Plivo at all, and any business's commercial call from an ordinary 10-digit number is prohibited under TRAI's rules however it is connected, so the one technically real route (the user's own SIM doing the dialling) solves only the engineering half. The owner's single requested change needs no voice, routing or dialplan code for a hand-provisioned pilot; turning it into a product needs a small set of platform changes that this report separates from everything optional. One controlled forward per operator, with a SIP capture on a spare DID, settles the largest unknowns, and no such call exists in our logs. Separately and more urgently, TRAI's A2P declaration rule takes effect around **17 November 2026, 14 days from this report**, and it applies to LesCall's existing outbound traffic whether or not this feature is ever built. Nothing has been built or changed, no test call was placed, and every legal point below needs counsel.

Evidence tags: **[V]** means read directly in our code, live config, retained logs or a regulator's own text. **[S]** means stated by a vendor, law firm or press source and not independently confirmed, so vendor rules are accurate as that vendor's statement. **[I]** means inference. **[U]** means unverified or unknown. Several web sources passed through a summarising fetcher, so re-open any quote before relying on its exact wording.

**Forwarding works on the platform without code because a forwarded call is the same SIP leg as any other.** It arrives at the same dialplan, codec and WebSocket format, and routing resolves the agent from `phone_numbers` by the DID's significant digits (`conversation_call_bridge.py:348-367`) [V, spot-checked]. Nothing in the code reads Diversion, History-Info or P-Asserted-Identity, so the personal number is invisible and a dedicated DID is what identifies the user [V]. On the carrier side the pattern is already commercial in India: hiRobin registers busy, no-reply and unreachable rules and never uses unconditional ([hiRobin](https://www.hirobin.ai/blog/call-forwarding-failures-india)), Exotel published a self-serve guide for forwarding a personal number to a voice agent in May 2026 ([Exotel](https://exotel.com/build/set-up-a-voice-agent-for-your-phone-number-in-an-afternoon/)), and Truecaller's free AI voicemail tells Indian users to enable forwarding ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/truecaller-launches-free-ai-powered-voicemail-for-android-with-12-indian-languages-507367-2025-12-19)) [S]. Everyone verified on the "own number" pitch uses forwarding or a device feature; nobody was found with a SIM-free, forwarding-free attachment in India.

**Conditional forwarding is also the honest fit for "direct assistant".** The no-answer timer runs in 5-second steps from 5 to 30 seconds with the phone ringing throughout ([Autobot](https://getautobot.app/blog/call-forwarding-codes)), and a declined call usually follows the busy rule ([hiRobin](https://www.hirobin.ai/blog/call-forwarding-failures-india)), which would give the user a "send it to the assistant" gesture [S, vendor only]. Three carrier facts complicate it. Callers may hear "your call has been forwarded" before the agent speaks on some operators, and Exotel's removal route needed corporate numbers and, on Airtel, postpaid plus a company letter ([Exotel, 2023](https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/135841-i-have-an-existing-phone-number-how-can-i-use-it-with-exotel-)) [S, stale]. iPhone Settings forward all calls only, so conditional rules need codes, and Exotel says Live Voicemail must be off or carrier forwarding never triggers ([Exotel](https://exotel.com/build/set-up-a-voice-agent-for-your-phone-number-in-an-afternoon/)) [S]. And rules fail silently: hiRobin's self-reported telemetry puts declined or missed calls that never arrived at Airtel 42.6%, Jio 30.7%, Vi 30.7% and BSNL 30.4%, with no sample size or window and from a vendor selling monitoring ([hiRobin](https://www.hirobin.ai/blog/call-forwarding-failures-india)) [S]. Those figures are not a clean failure rate, but they mean monitoring belongs in the product, not on a later list.

**The DID class matters.** Plivo's landline series (022, 080) is the right target, hiRobin's published targets are 080 numbers, and Jio's help text allows forwarding to mobiles and landlines except BSNL numbers, the user's own number and international numbers ([Jio](https://www.jio.com/help/faq/mobile/services/hd-voice/how-can-i-activate-call-forwarding-service/)) [S]. The 1600 and 140 series are defined for outbound use and should not be designed around [I]. Plivo's docs do not name the underlying carrier, but one retained Diversion sample carries the host `ka.ims.ttl.in`, which reads as a Tata Teleservices IMS rather than BSNL. That is my inference from a hostname on one DID [I], so the Jio exclusion still needs a test.

**Device routes stay narrow.** Android reserves `VOICE_CALL`, `VOICE_UPLINK` and `VOICE_DOWNLINK` for privileged apps holding `CAPTURE_AUDIO_OUTPUT`, which "is not available to third-party applications" ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaRecorder.AudioSource)), and the intercept path is `@SystemApi` ([AOSP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/main/telecomm/java/android/telecom/Call.java)) [S, official docs]. Play bans the Accessibility API for call recording ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170?hl=en)). The one unprivileged Android idea is a default-dialer app that merges a second cellular leg to a LesCall DID using public `conference()` calls, or `Call.transfer` on API 37 ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telecom/Call)). It has not been prototyped, it costs two airtime legs, and it requires users to replace their dialer [I]. iOS has no equivalent. Truecaller's recording falls back to a merged line because of CallKit limits ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/25/truecaller-brings-call-recording-and-transcription-to-india)), though "no public API" rests on that workaround because Apple's developer pages could not be read [I]. A Bluetooth HFP box works on both platforms in principle through standard stacks ([ESP-IDF](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32/api-reference/bluetooth/esp_hf_client.html), [Asterisk](https://docs.asterisk.org/Configuration/Channel-Drivers/Mobile-Channel/Mobile-Channel-Concepts/)), but it means one radio per phone within Bluetooth range and no commercial product was found.

**WhatsApp is real but answers a different question.** Meta publishes a FreeSWITCH-over-SIP guide using sofia, TLS, SDES and Opus ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/calling/integration-examples)), user-initiated calls are free ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/calling/pricing/)), and India is not on the business-initiated exclusion list ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/calling/)) [S, official]. But the number must be on Cloud API rather than an app, Meta's coexistence table excludes calling ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/)), and ordinary phone calls still ring the handset. So a real personal number would lose its WhatsApp app and the agent would cover only callers who choose WhatsApp [I]. That suits a business's second number, not a person's primary one.

## Outbound on the personal number is closed wherever a cloud trunk places the call

**Plivo refuses it in writing:** verified caller ID is "Not applicable" for India, "Indian regulations require using Plivo-rented Indian numbers as caller ID" ([Plivo](https://www.plivo.com/docs/voice/concepts/verified-caller-id.md)), and Twilio routes outbound to India only from non-Indian numbers ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/in/voice)) [S]. A +91 mobile CLI from a foreign route is what DoT's spoofed-call system was built to block ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/10/223-indian-spoofed-calls-prevention-system-blocks-1-35-crore-calls-amidst-false-positives-concerns/)) [S]. By statute, the strongest clauses are s.42(3)(b) and (c) of the Telecommunications Act 2023: up to 3 years, up to Rs 50 lakh, cognizable and non-bailable ([Act](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/Telecommunications_01012024.pdf)) [V for the text]. Whether a subscriber-consented CLI counts as tampering is untested, and the Act does not define the word. Consent helps on intent, but it does not obviously cure s.3(8)'s "allotted" language [I].

**Our code does not stop this, and that deserves attention.** Caller ID is simply the digits of `phone_numbers.number` placed in `origination_caller_id_number` (`dashboard/server.py:4825`, `shared/fs_originate.py:87-106`), and `POST /api/numbers` checks only 7+ digits, trunk ownership and agent ownership (`dashboard/telephony_api.py:381-400`) [V]. So a paid tenant could register a mobile today and the platform would try to present it. Whether Plivo's Zentrunk rejects or rewrites that was never tested [U]. The code comment that the tenant's own carrier authorises the CLI (`dashboard/server.py:4803-4807`) is also weaker than it reads, because the two gateway files inspected point at the same `zt.plivo.com` host, so those two "own" trunks are one carrier account [V].

**Routes where the SIM truly dials are a different matter, and "impossible" would overstate it.** A gateway, an HFP box or an Android conference would genuinely show the personal number, since it is a real SIM call [I]. They fail on the rules instead. TCCCPR reg 3 requires commercial communication to use registered headers or special-series numbers, and reg 2(bw) treats any commercial communication from an unregistered Sender as unsolicited ([TRAI 2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)) [V]. The first violation bars outgoing on **all** that person's telecom resources for 15 days, and the next disconnects them for a year with device blocking ([TRAI 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/Regulation_18092026.pdf)) [V]. For a user, that is the loss of their main phone number. An AI calling on an individual's non-commercial behalf (a dentist, family) is A2P on its face, TRAI says the rules "apply to all A2P calls", and the Sender definition could not be read, so that remains grey [I].

**What can be done outbound:** keep placing calls from a rented, KYC'd, declared number in the correct series, with the person's name in the spoken introduction. CNAP, ordered from 1 November 2026, shows the KYC name on 4G and newer ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/mobile-users-to-see-callers-names-under-new-cnap-framework-from-november-1)) [S], so recipients of a rented number may see LesCall or the tenant depending on who holds the KYC [I]. Ask Plivo which. A callback to a personal CLI would also ring the phone, not the agent, unless that mobile also forwards [I].

## The legal picture has one deadline and several open questions

**The deadline.** TRAI's TCCCPR amendment dated 18 September 2026 requires every Sender to declare A2P use and CLI ranges to the originating access provider in advance, and "any such call made by a Sender without prior declaration... shall be treated as Unsolicited commercial communication". Its A2P definition expressly includes "artificial voice", and TRAI rejected a live-agent exclusion ([TRAI 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/Regulation_18092026.pdf)) [V]. By my reading of clause 1(3), the general provisions started after 30 days (about 18 October), the declaration and termination-charge provisions start after 60 (**about 17 November 2026**), and the Annexure after 90 (about 17 December). A vendor notes the e-Gazette reference contains 28 September ([The Telephony Co](https://www.thetelephony.co/india/blog/tcccpr-third-amendment-dates)), which would move these to about 28 October, 27 November and 27 December [S]. Plan on the earlier date.

The stakes are shared across tenants. TRAI says "whole PRI/SIP links need to be barred, and not individual DID numbers", and in April 2026, 15,736 of 20,591 disconnections were SIP/PRI connections used for A2P calls ([TRAI 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/Regulation_18092026.pdf)) [V]. One tenant's complaints could therefore cost every tenant the shared trunk [I]. The research did not establish whether LesCall has declared, whether Plivo has a process, which series the five live numbers use, or whether current outbound traffic is promotional. TRAI's own memorandum and Plivo's landline offer sit in tension with a strict reading of reg 3(1), so non-BFSI service calls on regular numbers look tolerated for now and may narrow [I]. **Inbound forwarding creates no outbound Sender event, so this deadline should not reach the forwarded leg** [I, no text says so expressly]. It is a separate action item, and it is the most time-critical thing in this report.

**Later dates.** Real-time SIM-count checks by 30 November 2026 ([Mondaq via Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/corporate-commercial-law/overview-of-telecom-user-identification-guidelines)) [S]; India WhatsApp accounts move to INR billing by 31 December 2026 ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/)) [S]; SIM-binding compliance reportedly 31 December 2026 (dates conflict, below); DPDP substantive duties from 13 May 2027 ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)) [S].

**Forwarding itself.** No instrument found prohibits a subscriber forwarding their own line to a business DID, and none expressly permits it, so the verdict rests on an absence [I]. DoT's order of 28 March 2024 told licensees to "discontinue the existing USSD-based call forwarding services" from 15 April 2024 "till further notice", naming *401# unconditional forwarding as the misused service ([Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/dot-directs-deactivation-of-ussd-based-call-forwarding-by-april-15/)) [S, press only; the DoT PDF returned 404]. No notice lifting it was found. Treat it as in force, and do not build onboarding on *401# or on the "dial this code" gesture that DoT warns about ([News On Air](https://newsonair.gov.in/government-issues-advisory-against-calls-impersonating-dot-threatening-people-to-disconnect-mobile-numbers)). Three softer constraints follow. Operator plan terms reportedly limit benefits to personal, non-commercial use, which is a contract risk rather than an offence, sourced only to old or digest-level pages ([Trak.in](https://trak.in/stories/airtels-unlimited-5g-usage-is-limited-to-300gb-in-30-days-no-commercial-data-usage-allowed/), [TechPP](https://techpp.com/2017/10/13/reliance-jio-terms-conditions-opinion/)) [U]. Forwarded calls can carry voice OTPs ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/new-cyber-scam-trick-asks-you-to-dial-a-code-beware/amp)), and our pipeline would transcribe and store them [I]. And DPDP applies to callers who never chose LesCall: a business user is the likely Data Fiduciary with LesCall as Processor, but s.3(c)(i) exempts an individual's personal or domestic processing ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)) [V for the text], which could leave LesCall as the only entity with duties to those callers [I]. On recording, no statute setting a general private-recording rule was found, and the reported spousal cases conflict and are weak guides for a commercial recorder.

**SIM boxes are the clearest no.** Possessing radio equipment beyond a notified SIM count is an offence under s.42(3)(d), unauthorised service provision carries up to Rs 2 crore under s.42(1), and using such a service knowingly is exposed under the Third Schedule, which makes it a bad thing to sell ([Act](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/Telecommunications_01012024.pdf)) [V, column mapping from a two-column PDF extraction]. TCCCPR Schedule IV names "SIM Farm/SIM box type usage" as a detection target ([TRAI 2025](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/Regulation_12022025.pdf)) [V]. The notified threshold was not found, and that number decides the single-SIM case [U].

**Questions for counsel:** whether forwarding-based inbound for business users is "providing telecommunication services"; whether a subscriber-consented CLI falls under s.42(3)(b)/(c); the s.42(3)(d) threshold and any authorisation need for a single-SIM gateway; whether LesCall is a Reseller, OSP or TIUE; who is the Sender and principal entity when a tenant's agent calls; whether personal-use A2P falls under reg 4; DPDP roles for third-party callers, especially under consumer users; recording notice wording; whether PSTN-to-PSTN handoff raises toll bypass; and whether the ss.33-34 civil-penalty provisions are in force. Counsel should read the texts the research could not obtain: TCCCPR 2018 principal regulations, TRAI's 13 August 2024 Direction, OSP Guidelines 2021, Principal Authorisation Rules 2026, Cyber Security Amendment Rules 2025, User Identification Rules 2026, the SIM-binding Directions, the DoT *401# order and Amendment 01/Licensing Policy/2026.

## One spare DID and four SIMs settle most of the open questions

**No real forwarded call exists in our logs.** The 18 retained header dumps (30 October to 3 November) are all sentinel self-probes [V]. `variable_sip_h_Diversion` appeared on 6 of 18, on one DID, shaped `reason="tgrp-reroute"` and naming a number that was not the DID, on calls that were not forwarded [V]. That is a warning in itself: **a Diversion header does not mean "forwarded", and its user part does not mean "subscriber"**. A router must match it against a registered source and treat a non-match as not routable [I]. Plivo's and Twilio's docs both call forwarded-from information carrier-dependent ([Plivo](https://docs.plivo.com/docs/voice/xml/overview.md), [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/twiml)) [S], and no public capture for India was found.

**The test design needs one correction.** The forwarding note assumes a spare DID "not bound to a live agent" keeps testers away from an agent. On this platform it does not: the catch-all answers everything [V]. An **unregistered** DID reaches the founder's active agent, while a **registered row with no inbound agent** answers with the neutral fallback (`conversation_call_bridge.py:377-381`). So register the row, leave the agent empty, set `direction='inbound'` so the probe skips it, and expect one false silence alert after the baseline call unless excluded. No code is needed: `socket_server.py:1932-1933` already debug-logs every CHANNEL_DATA header, and `sofia global siptrace on` or `sngrep` adds the wire view. Copy the results out promptly, because the retained logs cover only about five days. Note that a capture at FreeSWITCH cannot distinguish a header the carrier never sent from one Plivo stripped, so ask Plivo support in parallel.

## The recommended path runs in five steps

First, independent of this feature and before about 17 November, confirm the A2P declaration position with Plivo and counsel, including the CLI ranges and the series of the five live numbers. Second, run T1 to T6 on a spare DID. Third, put counsel's first question (is forwarding-based inbound "providing telecommunication services"?) and the Reseller question in front of a telecom lawyer before charging anyone. Fourth, pilot Group A with one or two business users who hold their own KYC documents, on conditional forwarding through the operator app or Settings, with the direction-label fix shipped first and the AI and recording notice in the opening line if counsel agrees. Fifth, build Group B only if the pilot's rules hold under T7, and treat the shared DID as a later cost decision that depends on T1 plus the `:5080` gate. Throughout, keep outbound on rented numbers, never put a mobile in `phone_numbers`, and tell users plainly that a LesCall number sits behind their forward and how to cancel it (`##002#`, or `*413` on Jio [S, T3 to confirm]). Keep the DID reserved through a grace period after cancellation, so a forgotten forward does not land on a dead line or a stranger's agent.

## Conclusion

The owner framed this as attaching the agent to a personal number instead of a Plivo number, but the research shows the two are not alternatives: in India the personal number is the front door and a rented DID remains the only practical thing behind it. The feature therefore changes less in the platform than the framing suggests. The real decisions are commercial and regulatory: who holds the KYC, whether one DID per user survives consumer pricing, and how to tell a user that a stranger's unanswered call may now be recorded by an AI on their behalf.

The audit's most useful result is that the riskiest options look cheapest in code. A personal CLI is a few lines and a gateway is a config file, while the lawful path needs a pool, a health check and a careful onboarding flow. Our own API will accept a mobile number as a CLI today, so code review alone will not keep the platform on the right side of this. The rule has to be stated as policy, and the A2P date makes the wider question of how LesCall's outbound calls are declared more pressing than the feature that prompted this research.
